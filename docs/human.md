# The human

Before any pixel: a person has spent a lifetime with mirrors. The guideline
starts here, not with the system.

## What people already do with mirrors

- **Rehearse.** Goffman (*The Presentation of Self in Everyday Life*, 1959):
  social life is performance; the mirror is the backstage where the
  performance is prepared. People practice faces, postures, words. A smart
  mirror enters a rehearsal room, not a blank screen.
- **Recognize.** Gallup (1970; Amsterdam 1972 in humans): self-recognition
  around 18 months. The mirror is the first technology that ever showed a
  human being itself. That primacy commands a trust no app has earned.
- **Confess.** People talk to mirrors, cry at them, psych themselves up.
  The mirror is the one witness that never judges, never repeats, never
  scores. Every smart feature that judges (skin scores, posture grades,
  wellness indices) spends this trust. Spend it knowingly or not at all.
- **Glance.** Most mirror encounters last seconds: teeth, hair, outfit,
  out the door. The modal encounter is not a session. It is a glance.

## Consequences for design

1. **The mirror was innocent.** A conventional mirror cannot betray you.
   Ours can — camera, microphone, scores, cloud. Every sensing capability
   must justify itself against the innocence it removes (derived — Goffman
   backstage; Marky et al. 2020 bystander awareness).
2. **Never grade the face uninvited.** A score that appears without asking
   converts a witness into a judge. Scores appear on request, with context,
   and leave with one tap (proposed — needs validation, but the DeviceDaily
   headline on HiMirror, "focuses on your flaws", is the warning label).
3. **Rituals outrank features.** Morning routine order (teeth → face →
   outfit) is older than any OS. Fit features into ritual order; never
   reorder a ritual around a feature (derived — calm technology: dwell
   with computers, don't interact at them).
4. **The reflection is the senior partner.** UI is the guest. When in doubt,
   remove (derived — HIG Deference).
